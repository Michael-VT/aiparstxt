Demand for IPv4 in 2026 is shifting, not declining.

The market isn't driven solely by headlines about hyperscale projects or large-scale speculative purchases as it once was. We're now seeing healthier behavior in the market: broader demand for infrastructure, more disciplined purchasing behavior, and sustained operational use across the hosting, cloud, broadband, and AI ecosystems.

Even with lower prices in some segments, data transfer activity and address request volumes remain high. This is significant. It demonstrates that IPv4 remains a critical production asset, enabling real-world services, customer connectivity, CG-NAT environments, edge infrastructure, and globally routed workloads.

The rise of AI is also quietly strengthening the market foundation. More computing power, more distributed infrastructure, more cloud computing—all of this remains heavily dependent on IPv4 compatibility in real-world deployments. And, oddly enough, at the same time, apparently due to past experience, broadband expansion programs and network modernization projects continue to create long-term demand for addresses.

The buyer structure is changing.

The IPv4 market is becoming more diversified, more infrastructure-focused, and less emotionally charged. Instead of short-term hype cycles, we are seeing a leveling off and steady demand for clean resources and growing activity from hosting providers and operators working in adjacent cloud environments. Perhaps this is simply easier to understand and more secure.

This is a positive sign for the industry.

A mature market doesn't need panic buying to stay healthy. It requires stable utility, real deployment pressure, and long-term operational relevance—and IPv4 continues to demonstrate all three.
