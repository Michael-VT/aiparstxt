The reliability and resilience of European data centers is being tested amid the AI ​​boom.
Recent incidents highlight ongoing infrastructure challenges in Europe. On May 7, 2026, a fire broke out at the NorthC data center near Amsterdam. Simultaneously, Zscaler reported the degradation of its Amsterdam II facility (May 6-7), and power issues at AWS in Northern Virginia spread globally.
These events highlight the vulnerability of the power supply and the increasing demands for business continuity at critical moments. Data centers already consume approximately 3% of the EU's electricity, and a sharp increase is expected as demand for AI accelerates.
Nevertheless, the outlook remains positive. The EU is advancing the Cloud and AI Development Act, expected to be passed this May, which aims to triple data center capacity. While grid congestion and local restrictions pose obstacles, these steps demonstrate a strategic commitment to scaling digital infrastructure. For investors and operators, the message is clear: there are short-term risks associated with incidents and power supply constraints, but active political support and innovation in efficient cooling/power solutions point to strong long-term opportunities in European digital infrastructure. And ongoing work in this area shows encouraging trends.
